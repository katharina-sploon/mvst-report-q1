Q1 Summary

Q1 showed consistent output and steady growth across all key metrics. Comments trended upward throughout the quarter — a positive signal that content is generating more genuine interaction. The December engagement spike (45.8% native rate) was largely driven by team-tagged offsite content and is not representative of typical performance.

The more relevant pattern: impressions remain relatively stable regardless of posting volume, which suggests that reach is increasingly tied to content quality and resonance rather than frequency alone. This is the right foundation to build on in Q2.

One area to address going forward: the current audience is primarily reaching talent, potential new team members, and industry peers — which has clear value for employer branding and partnerships, but represents a gap when it comes to reaching prospective clients and decision-makers. Shifting that balance is a key focus for Q2.

Audience — Where We Stand & Where We're Heading

The current audience is concentrated in the tech and software space, which reflects the type of content that has performed best so far — team moments, culture posts, and behind-the-scenes content consistently outperform more in-depth technical or strategic pieces. That's a natural platform dynamic and not something to fight against entirely.

That said, the entry-level skew (40.8%) and the tech-heavy industry mix indicate that we're not yet effectively reaching the senior decision-makers and client verticals we want to attract in Q2. The shift will happen through a combination of approaches: repurposing high-performing content from Niklas and Philipp's personal profiles onto the company page, deliberately targeting industries like Healthcare, Manufacturing, and Financial Services in both content themes and invite strategy, and using targeted LinkedIn page follow invites from Niklas and Philipp's profiles to gradually increase the share of Directors, Owners, and VPs in our audience.

What the Latest LinkedIn Algorithm Tells Us

The algorithm has shifted meaningfully. Here's what the data currently shows — and what it means for how we post.

2–4 Images

Posts with 2–4 images outperform single-image posts. A single image results in approximately −10% reach and −15% engagement.

1,400–1,800

Optimal character count for text-only posts. Publishing two text-only posts back to back causes around −25% reach loss on the second post.

10:00–14:00

Best window for high engagement quality in Europe. Posting between 07:00–09:00 can capture early attention with less competition.

7–11 Slides

Sweet spot for carousel / document posts, paired with 300–400 characters of accompanying text for best reach.

30 Min

Respond to every comment within 30 minutes of publishing. Doing so increases comments by +64% and multiplies views by 2.3×.

30–50%

Of active LinkedIn time should go toward commenting on others' posts — not just publishing. Engagement drives distribution.

3.4×

Conversion rate drops 3.4× when links appear in post comments on conversion-focused posts. Avoid links in comments where the goal is action.

−45% Reach

Posting every day reduces reach by up to 45%. 2–3 high-quality posts per week per profile is the recommended cadence.

What This Means in Practice

More posting is not the answer — and the data now confirms it. 2–3 quality posts per week per founder profile, combined with active community engagement (commenting, responding, thoughtful reposts), will outperform a higher-volume approach with less interaction.

Reposts are also a signal to the algorithm that you're engaged with your network. A short, contextual repost — "Worth reading because…" — counts as content and shows up in feeds. We'll build this into the weekly rhythm: 2 original posts + 1 repost per founder per week, with comment responses prioritised in the first 30 minutes after publishing.

My Take — Instagram

Instagram performs well when the content is genuinely human and unforced. The strongest posts have always been the ones where the team is naturally present — not staged, not polished for the sake of it. That dynamic is hard to manufacture, and it shouldn't be.

What works: candid team moments, real product work in context, people giving their take on something they actually care about. BTS from the office, reusing content that already landed on LinkedIn, and weekly Stories showing what's going on day to day. The more the team is naturally willing to be in front of the camera, the better this channel performs — it can't be forced, but it can be made easy.

06 How We Work Together — Q2 Collaboration

To consistently produce content that reflects what MVST is actually doing — and to plan ahead rather than react — there are a few areas where closer collaboration would make a meaningful difference.

Keep us in the loop on partner visits and events

Right now we often find out about partner visits, client trips, or events after the fact — or by chance. To plan content around these moments, we need a heads-up in advance. A short message when something is confirmed is enough. The more lead time we have, the better the content we can build around it.

Let us know when projects can be talked about

We'd love to create more client-focused content, but we need to know when projects are at a stage where they can be shared publicly — and what level of detail is appropriate. A brief check-in when a project wraps or reaches a milestone would help us plan case study content in advance.

Define the competitors we should be watching

We're currently benchmarking against COBE and DieProduktMacher based on follower count and posting volume. If there are other agencies or studios you consider more relevant comparators, please flag them — we'll add them to the reporting going forward.

Share relevant blog posts before they go live

Not every post needs to link to a blog article, and social media serves an awareness function that doesn't always require a direct click-through. That said, when a blog post is going live, it helps to know in advance so we can align social content timing. A brief heads-up — even a day before — makes a difference.

Content creation runs fully proactively from our side

For Niklas and Philipp's profiles, we have clear content pillars defined for Q2. No additional briefing or input is needed from their side before drafting — we produce posts proactively and submit them for approval. The one thing that matters most is timely approval, so posts can go live on schedule. Consistent posting depends on a consistent review cycle.

Share the broader MVST strategy focus when it shifts

The more aligned our content is with where MVST is heading commercially — which verticals are in focus, what services are being pushed, what conversations are happening in sales — the more effective the social output will be. A regular touchpoint, even a short one, helps us calibrate.
